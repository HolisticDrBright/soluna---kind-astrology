# Chinese & Eastern Astrology Primer

*A Soluna-owned reference. Original content, written for warmth and reflection — not prediction.*

## How Soluna holds Eastern astrology — and an important honesty

Chinese astrology is an old, rich tradition. Soluna draws on a small, respectful slice of it as a **reflective lens** — a gentle mirror, never a fixed fate.

**Read this part carefully, because it shapes everything below.** A full Eastern reading (such as a BaZi or "Four Pillars" chart) uses the year, month, day, *and hour* of birth, weaving together many elements and animals into a detailed picture. **Soluna does not do that.** Soluna only knows a person's **birth year**, which gives us three high-level signals:

1. the **year animal** (one of the twelve zodiac animals),
2. the **year element** (one of the five elements), and
3. the **yin or yang polarity** of the year.

So everything Soluna offers from this system is intentionally broad and high-level. We never claim it is a complete Eastern chart, and we never let a single year-sign decide anything about a person's life or relationships. When Soluna speaks here, it speaks softly, names the limits, and treats each signal as an archetype to try on — not a truth about who someone is.

## The twelve zodiac animals

The animal of your birth year is an archetype — a flavor of temperament with a strength and a growth edge. Soluna presents each warmly and refuses the old harsh stereotypes (no "sneaky," no "lazy," no "reckless by nature").

| Animal | Archetype in brief | Growth edge |
|---|---|---|
| Rat | Quick, observant, resourceful; thinks a few steps ahead | Can overthink or worry about scarcity |
| Ox | Steady, patient, dependable; finishes what it starts | Can resist change or forget to rest |
| Tiger | Bold, passionate, courageous; leads and protects | Can act before pausing |
| Rabbit | Gentle, gracious, peace-loving; creates calm | Can avoid needed conflict |
| Dragon | Visionary, confident, charismatic; inspires big dreams | Can skip follow-through |
| Snake | Wise, intuitive, reflective; senses what's beneath | Can become guarded or overanalyze |
| Horse | Free-spirited, energetic, adventurous; happiest in motion | Can struggle with restlessness |
| Goat | Tender, creative, compassionate; loves beauty | Can be sensitive to criticism |
| Monkey | Clever, playful, inventive; improvises and learns fast | Can get restless or skip depth |
| Rooster | Observant, honest, orderly; takes pride in doing well | Can be perfectionistic or self-critical |
| Dog | Loyal, honest, protective; a steadfast friend | Can worry or expect the worst |
| Pig | Warm, sincere, generous; easy to be around | Can give too much or avoid hard truths |

Each animal is a starting point for reflection — Soluna names the gift first, then offers the growth edge as an invitation, never a flaw.

## The five elements

Chinese tradition layers an **element** over the animal, giving the year a further flavor. Because Soluna works from the birth year only, we describe the element as an *energetic flavor* of a person or season, not a full elemental analysis.

- **Wood** — growth-oriented and expansive, like a tree reaching upward. Carries vision, idealism, and the drive to begin and build. Growth edge: can overextend or get rigid when blocked. *Tags: learning_growth, creativity.*
- **Fire** — bright, passionate, and expressive; warmth that draws people in. Carries enthusiasm and the spark to inspire. Growth edge: can burn hot then burn out. *Tags: action_initiative, stress_regulation.*
- **Earth** — grounded, nurturing, and steady; the stable center that holds things together. Carries patience and a gift for security. Growth edge: can over-give or resist change for comfort's sake. *Tags: nurture_care, planning_structure.*
- **Metal** — clear, disciplined, and refined, like a well-honed edge. Carries structure, integrity, and a love of quality. Growth edge: can become rigid or perfectionistic. *Tags: planning_structure, decision_clarity.*
- **Water** — fluid, intuitive, and deep; flowing around obstacles to find the quiet path. Carries adaptability and reflective wisdom. Growth edge: can absorb others' moods or drift without a clear shore. *Tags: change_release, stress_regulation.*

A useful image: the animal is *who* shows up, and the element is the *weather* they bring with them. A Wood Tiger and a Water Tiger share a brave heart but move through the world differently.

## Yin and yang

Each year also carries a **polarity** — yin or yang. Soluna frames these as two complementary currents, never as a hierarchy where one is better than the other.

- **Yin** is the receptive, inward, restful current — the energy of listening, gathering, and softening. It is *not* weakness; it is the wisdom of pausing, receiving, and replenishing. A yin flavor invites slowing down and letting things unfold. *Tags: rest_recovery, nurture_care.*
- **Yang** is the active, outward, expressive current — the energy of doing, initiating, and moving forward. It is *not* aggression; it is the spark that brings ideas into the world. A yang flavor invites stepping up and taking action. *Tags: action_initiative.*

Soluna's framing: yin and yang are a pair of rhythms, both needed, each wise in its own season. Neither is a verdict on a person's strength.

## Compatibility: different rhythms, never doom

Eastern tradition groups the twelve animals into **harmony sets** — often called *trines* — where the animals in a group tend to share an easy, natural rhythm with one another. It also notes pairings whose styles differ and may take more translation.

Soluna treats this with real care. We describe compatibility as **different rhythms**, not as fate:

- Some bonds feel naturally *in sync* — an easy shared pace.
- Others ask for a little *translation* — two different tempos learning each other's beat.
- **Different rhythms are not doom.** Friction between styles is simply difference, never a sentence that two people shouldn't try.

The harmony groupings are high-level and never deterministic. A real relationship depends on the two people in it — their care, their communication, their choices — far more than on the labels of their birth years. Soluna would never say "you two are incompatible" or "certain signs are doomed together." Instead it might say: "your styles may move at different paces — here's a place curiosity could help." *Tags: connection_love, communication.*

## Interpretation rules for personal guidance

This section governs how Soluna turns Eastern astrology into kind, practical guidance — and, just as importantly, how it stays honest about its limits.

### Stay within what the birth year can tell us

This is the first rule, and it overrides the rest. Soluna only has the **year animal, year element, and yin/yang polarity.** That means:

- **Never overclaim.** Do not imply a full Eastern, BaZi, or Four-Pillars reading. Say clearly, when relevant, that this is a high-level lens based on birth year alone.
- **Keep it broad.** Offer archetypal flavors and gentle reflection, not specific predictions about a person's life, year, or relationships.
- **Pair it with humility.** A simple line like "based on your birth year only, as a gentle lens" keeps Soluna honest.

### Synthesis tags: how this system connects to the others

Each animal, element, and polarity above maps to one or more **synthesis tags** — a shared, controlled vocabulary that lets Eastern astrology speak the same language as Western astrology and numerology. The tags are: `communication`, `action_initiative`, `rest_recovery`, `connection_love`, `boundaries`, `work_focus`, `change_release`, `self_worth`, `creativity`, `decision_clarity`, `timing_patience`, `grief_processing`, `stress_regulation`, `planning_structure`, `freedom_independence`, `nurture_care`, `learning_growth`, and `leadership`.

These tags are the bridge between systems. When a Tiger year (`action_initiative`, `leadership`) lines up with a Mars placement (`action_initiative`) and a Life Path 1 (`leadership`), Soluna can speak with a little more warmth and confidence, because several lenses agree. When a yang polarity (`action_initiative`) meets a user who says they're depleted (`rest_recovery`), Soluna **holds the tension and softens**, honoring the person over the label.

### How to turn an Eastern signal into guidance

1. **Lead with the gift, then the growth edge.** Animal and element strengths come first; edges are offered as gentle invitations.
2. **Use the element as weather and the animal as character.** Combine them lightly for texture, not as a precise formula.
3. **Frame yin/yang as a season's rhythm**, suggesting whether it may be a time to act or a time to receive — never as a rule.
4. **Treat compatibility as curiosity.** Different rhythms open a conversation; they never close one.
5. **Let agreement across systems raise confidence**, and let the birth-year-only limit keep the overall tone humble.

### What to avoid

- **Never claim a full Eastern chart.** This is the cardinal rule of this system.
- **Never predict events or outcomes** from a year animal, element, or polarity.
- **Never use compatibility as doom.** No "incompatible," no "doomed together."
- **Never revive the harsh stereotypes.** No "sneaky" Rat, "lazy" Pig, "reckless" Tiger, "cold" Snake. Name the same energy with kindness.
- **Never rank yin below yang** (or vice versa). Both are wise.
- **Never claim scientific certainty.** This is a reflective lens, and Soluna says so.

The goal is always the same: a person should meet their year animal and feel gently *seen* — offered a warm archetype and a small, kind invitation — never reduced to a label, never warned, never told their relationships or future are already decided.
